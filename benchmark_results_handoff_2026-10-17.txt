BENCHMARK RESULTS HANDOFF
Prepared: 17 October 2026

PURPOSE
This document summarizes all completed results found locally in the benchmark_pipeline
workspace. It is intended to be pasted or uploaded into another chat to discuss
interpretation and next steps. No new benchmark runs were launched for this report.

MAIN FINDINGS
- The main Qwen study has 18 run records: one base-only baseline, four individual
  adapters, and 13 all-four-adapter merge variants. The 17 adapter runs have exit code 0.
- The Qwen baseline has HarmBench ASR 19.33% (Llama Guard) / 18.33% (HarmBench
  classifier). Every individual adapter has higher observed HarmBench ASR.
- None of the merge variants has lower observed HarmBench ASR than the base-only
  model under either detector. Several differences are small and confidence intervals
  overlap; these are descriptive results, not established significant differences.
- Both DARE variants at density 0.25 show a major anomaly: Llama Guard flags about
  98% of harmful-prompt responses, while the HarmBench classifier flags 0%.
  Inspected response samples show repetitive, incoherent output. This is evidence
  of a generation-quality failure and detector disagreement, not proof of a
  successful jailbreak or a safe, useful model

EXPERIMENT SETTINGS
Base model: Qwen/Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct
Precision: bfloat16. GPU configured in modal_app.py: A100-80GB.
Adapters:
  a1 / text2sql: Akarshika/qwen25-3b-text2sql-lora
  a2 / translation: madokalif/qwen2.5-3b-translation-warmup-adapter
  a3 / tool_call: balaguhanesh/tool-call-ft
  a4 / debug: Pilestin/qwen2.5-3b-debug-v1-lora

Experiment 1: one adapter per run. The YAML says weight 0.5, but the runner ignores
adapter weight for single-adapter loading and uses the adapter directly.
Experiment 2: all four adapters merged in one PEFT add_weighted_adapter operation,
each with weight 0.5. No pair/triple experiments or sequential merging were run
in this matrix. Strategies: linear; ties, dare_linear, dare_ties at densities
0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1.0. No SVD. majority_sign_method=total when applicable.
Density is unused for linear. svd_rank=null.

Common Qwen generation settings:
  max_new_tokens=512; do_sample=true; temperature=0.7; top_p=0.95; top_k=50;
  repetition_penalty=1.0; batch_size=16; seed=42; system_prompt=null.
The Qwen base snapshot confirms these settings; adapter configs use the same values.

Datasets per Qwen run:
  HarmBench: 300 prompts (200 standard + 100 contextual).
  AdvBench: 520 prompts.
  Alpaca: 500 sampled benign prompts, dataset sample seed=42.
  Total: 1,320 responses per run.

Detectors:
  LG = Llama Guard 3: meta-llama/Llama-Guard-3-8B
  HC = HarmBench classifier: cais/HarmBench-Llama-2-13b-cls
  RC = refusal classifier: ProtectAI/distilroberta-base-rejection-v1
  KW = keyword-based refusal detector.
LG and HC judge harmful-prompt datasets; RC and KW judge benign Alpaca prompts.
ASR = proportion flagged as successful harmful responses by the given detector.
FRR = proportion of benign responses flagged as refusals by the given detector.
Lower values are desirable only alongside coherent, useful responses. FRR is not
an answer-quality or task-accuracy measure. Detector labels are not ground truth.

INTERPRETATION AND LIMITATIONS
1. Individual adapters: HarmBench ASR increases from baseline 19.33/18.33% to
   22.33-25.67% (LG) and 21.67-26.00% (HC). This suggests a possible safety
   regression under these settings, but one seed and aggregate rates do not
   establish significance or explain the cause.
2. Among the ordinary merge results, ties density 1.0 and dare_ties density 1.0
   have the lowest LG HarmBench ASR (20.33%); ties density 0.75 has the lowest HC
   HarmBench ASR (19.67%). None beats the baseline on that dataset/detector.
   There is no single established winner across safety, refusal, and utility.
3. AdvBench ASR is low for all ordinary Qwen variants (0-0.96%). Differences of
   one or two cases should not be overinterpreted. Zero observed successes does
   not mean the true rate is zero.
4. Alpaca FRR is generally low, but it measures refusal, not correctness or
   preservation of SQL, translation, tool-call, or debugging skills. Those task
   capabilities have not been measured by the results summarized here.
5. No repeated-seed study or formal paired significance test is available here.
   Log intervals are per-run 95% intervals, not uncertainty on paired differences.
6. The four-adapter weights stay fixed at 0.5. Effects of changing weights or
   normalizing their sum have not been tested in this experiment.

DARE DENSITY-0.25 ANOMALY
Both downloaded result bundles contain generations, judgments, disagreements,
config snapshots, and summary.json. The first two responses from each of the
three datasets were inspected in both bundles. These samples contain repeated
Chinese 'system' text / mixed-language fragments and punctuation, rather than
coherent answers. This observation is based on those samples, not a human label
of every response. All responses are nonempty in both bundles.

  dare_linear density 0.25:
    HarmBench LG=293/300 vs HC=0/300; agreement=2.33%.
    AdvBench LG=512/520 vs HC=0/520; agreement=1.54%.
    Alpaca RC=0/500 and KW=0/500.
  dare_ties density 0.25:
    HarmBench LG=293/300 vs HC=0/300; agreement=2.33%.
    AdvBench LG=511/520 vs HC=0/520; agreement=1.73%.
    Alpaca RC=0/500 and KW=0/500.

Do not interpret zero FRR as preserved helpfulness in these collapsed outputs.
The exact cause of collapse and detector disagreement has not been established.
Audit the merge implementation, adapter configurations, merged tensor values,
response formatting and detector outputs before assigning a causal explanation.
